AExecutive summary

The IA argues against the positioning

The actual problem

The current site's weakness is not that it is ugly. It is that the information architecture argues against the positioning.

A visitor who clicks "Work" — the single highest-intent action on a production studio site — is shown five service tiles with descriptive copy. The work is structurally prevented from ever being the hero. Every symptom the brief lists (small thumbnails, four per row, click-to-play, pagination, too much text, "feels like a freelancer") follows from a site organised around what the studio sells rather than what the studio made.

Three jobs, in priority order

  1. Structural — make "Work" mean work. Projects become the primary content type; services become filtered views over projects, not a parallel hierarchy. This alone fixes most of the brief.
  2. Positional — convert a six-person roster into a production company. More a content problem than a design problem, and it needs an honest conversation (risk 5).
  3. Technical — replatform without dropping the Arabic Reels page. Everything else is recoverable. That page is not, cheaply.

The bar

Not "the most technically impressive website." A visitor lands, sees moving food photography at scale within 400ms of paint, understands within one scroll that this is a crew with a client roster and a facility, and can name three brands Fraise has shot for without reading a paragraph.

BCore design direction

Black Room, Warm Plate

The interface has no colour of its own. Achromatic ground, bone-white type, hairline rules, mono metadata. Every pixel of chroma on the page comes from the food.

This is not a stylistic preference — it is the operative argument of the design, and it is what keeps the site from looking AI-generated:

  • It forbids gradients, glassmorphism, coloured blobs, tinted shadows and duotone overlays — not by taste, but because they would be the interface asserting colour it isn't entitled to.
  • It makes food photography read more saturated than it is, by contrast. A hero plate against #0B0B0C reads richer than the same plate on white or grey.
  • It matches the physical truth of the work: a grading suite, a blacked-out food studio, a cinema. The site should feel like the room where the work is finished.

Every generic-portfolio reflex gets answered by one question: does the interface earn colour here? The answer is no, except for one accent reserved for interactive state.

Positioning device — the slate

A film slate is the production industry's own metadata object. Every piece of media carries one:

JORDINA          TVC          2023          2.39:1

Mono, small, uppercase, tracked, bottom-inline-start of the frame. It does four jobs at once:

  1. Satisfies the client's explicit requirement — project and client name visible on every video — without captions that read like a portfolio.
  2. Reads as production infrastructure, not portfolio decoration. Freelancers write captions; productions write slates.
  3. Is locale-independent — client names, years and format notation are Latin/numeric in both AR and EN, so the slate survives RTL untouched.
  4. Gives every frame a typographic anchor, which is what stops a full-bleed video grid from reading as a moodboard.

Structural device — aspect ratio as taxonomy

The strongest single idea in this proposal. Each pillar has a native shape in the real world. Encode it:

  • The work grid becomes self-organising and asymmetric for a reason. A row pairing a 2.39:1 with a 9:16 is visually dynamic and semantically legible — the editorial asymmetry the brief asks for, without arbitrary art direction.
  • A visitor learns the taxonomy without reading service names. After one scroll, vertical = reels.
  • It is a hard constraint that makes every future layout decision easier and stops the grid drifting into card soup.

No card chrome. No rounded corners on media. Media is a frame — a sharp rectangle, radius 0, 2px maximum on controls only. Separation is hairline rules and whitespace, never shadows. Shadows imply floating cards; rules imply print.

CVisual language

Rules that hold the direction together

Ground. Near-black #0B0B0C, not #000. Pure black crushes against the letterboxed black of 2.39:1 video and erases the frame edge; it also renders as a hard OLED cutoff that looks cheap during fades.

Full-bleed is the default; margins are the exception. The inverse of a normal portfolio. Media goes edge to edge unless there is a compositional reason to inset it. Text is the thing that gets a container.

One h1 per page, and it is small. On a video-first site the largest typographic element should almost never be the page title. This is where most "premium studio" attempts fail — 120px type over the hero video reads as template instantly.

Numbering as structure. Keep the existing 01 / PEOPLE motif and apply it consistently — sections, projects, crew, BTS. Editorial pacing, free, already in the brand, culturally neutral across AR/EN.

Scrims, not opacity. Slate text sits on a bottom-anchored gradient scrim, never a reduced-opacity wash across the frame. Dulling the whole frame dulls the food, which contradicts the direction.

Motion is the only decoration. No illustration, no texture, no grain overlay, no noise. Grain in particular is the current AI-portfolio tell.

DHomepage architecture

Engineer the fold, don't default to 100vh

The client asked for "more videos visible when the page opens." The generic response is a 100vh hero video — which shows exactly one video and is the most common move on every reference site.

Recommendation

The hero occupies 88vh, not 100vh. The remaining ~12vh reveals the top edge of the first Selected Work row. It literally satisfies the request, proves scrollability without a bouncing chevron, is a deliberate compositional choice rather than a default, and costs nothing. Mobile uses 82svh so browser chrome can't crop it.

Client wall — set names in type, not logos

Do not build a logo grid. Three reasons, in order of weight:

  1. A grid of greyscale client logos is the SaaS "Trusted by" pattern — the fastest way to make a cinema-grade site look like a startup landing page.
  2. Logo quality will be inconsistent. KFC's mark is a full-colour illustrated portrait; a local halloumi brand's is likely a plain wordmark. Normalising to greyscale flattens KFC's recognition and makes the small brands look like placeholders.
  3. Names set at 48–72px in the site's own display face read as editorial credit — like a film's end titles — and stay on-brand.
KFC        ZAITY        JORDINA        BAKER
SUNWHITE   JOSWEET      THURAYA        UMIC

If the client insists on logos, the fallback is a logo revealed on hover over the typographic name — but argue for type first.

Deliberately not on the homepage

No testimonial carousel. No process-step diagram (that belongs on service pages). No awards badge row. No blog teaser. No numbers counting up on scroll — animated counters are a startup tell; numbers are set, not animated.

Reels is the exception

The Arabic Reels page is the strongest organic page in Search Console, so it is the most copy-rich of the five — the one page where I argue against minimal text. Good SEO and good art direction happen to coincide here.

FPortfolio interaction model

Kill pagination, schedule the whitespace

Numbered pagination is a listing convention borrowed from e-commerce and blogs. It fragments a body of work into arbitrary chunks and signals "there is a lot of this, sorted."

Replace with an explicit "Load more" button — not infinite scroll. Infinite scroll makes the footer (where contact lives) unreachable, reads as a feed rather than a curated reel, and hurts both INP and crawlability. A button is user-initiated: better interaction latency, better data behaviour on mobile, and the first N stay in server-rendered HTML.

The editorial cadence

Not a uniform grid, and not random asymmetry — a repeating four-row rhythm. A system, which is precisely the argument against "AI-generated template".

ROW B   ███████████████ ██████████████   2 × 16:9
ROW C   ████████████████     ███         1 × large offset + 1 × 9:16, whitespace inline-end
ROW D   ██████ ██████ ██████             3 × 4:5 stills
  • Never four videos in a row. Maximum three, and only for 4:5 stills.
  • Maximum two moving-image pieces per row.
  • Row C's whitespace is mandatory, not optional — the breathing room the brief asks for, scheduled rather than sprinkled.

Filters

A horizontal rail: ALL · TVC · RECIPES · REELS · STILLS · MENU. Filtering updates the URL via shallow routing (/work?service=reels) so a filtered view is shareable — but canonical always points to /work. The five pillar pages are the indexable filtered views. This avoids five thin, near-duplicate parameterised pages competing with the pillars.

GVideo interaction strategy

The section that decides premium vs broken

Two encodes per project, minimum

EncodePurposeSpec
Preview loopGrid hover / in-view autoplay6–8s, silent, audio track stripped entirely, ~720p max (640px wide for grid tiles), H.264 + optional AV1/WebM, target < 600 KB, faststart atom at head
Full filmLightbox / project pageAdaptive HLS via streaming provider, with audio and captions

Stripping the audio track isn't cosmetic — it saves real bytes, removes autoplay-policy edge cases entirely, and eliminates a whole bug class around muted video unmuting.

Posters must come from the video's own first frame

If the poster is a separately art-directed still, the poster→video transition visibly jumps and the whole effect reads cheap. This is the single most common failure mode of hover-preview grids.

Desktop

poster (next/image, no <video> src yet)
  → pointerenter
  → 120ms intent delay          prevents thrash on fast mouse sweeps
  → attach src, load, play()
  → crossfade poster → video, 240ms
  → pointerleave
  → pause, currentTime = 0, crossfade back, 300ms
  → keep src attached ~30s           re-hover is instant
  → detach src when scrolled out of viewport

The intent delay is not optional. Without it, dragging the cursor diagonally across a six-tile grid fires six load() calls and the browser thrashes.

Concurrency cap: maximum two decoding videos at any time (hero + one preview). A module-level manager pauses the least-recent when a third starts — mobile Safari has a hard simultaneous-decoder limit and degrades unpredictably past it. The hero pauses when scrolled out of viewport; a hero looping off-screen all session is pure battery and decode cost.

Mobile

Recommendation

Autoplay in viewport, but exactly one at a time — whichever card is nearest viewport centre.

Tap-to-preview was rejected: on a site whose entire complaint is "not enough video visible", a mobile default state of zero motion defeats the brief, and most users would never tap. Autoplay everything in view was rejected: three or four simultaneous decoders on a mid-range Android is jank plus real data cost — a meaningful concern in this market.

One-at-a-time centred autoplay gives the feeling of a live, motion-rich page for the cost of a single decoder. Bail out to posters only when prefers-reduced-motion, saveData, or effectiveType is 2g.

Keyboard parity

The hover preview must also fire on :focus-visible. A keyboard user tabbing the grid gets what a mouse user gets. One line, and almost every site with this pattern gets it wrong. The preview <video> is decorative and carries aria-hidden="true"; the wrapping link carries the accessible name.

Rules that keep it intentional

JTypography

Two scripts, two deliberate faces

A "supports Arabic and Latin" superfamily is the lazy answer and it always compromises one script. Choose two faces and match them optically.

RolePaid recommendationFree, production-viableAvoid
LatinSöhne · Suisse Int'lGeist SansInter, Poppins, Montserrat, DM Sans
Arabic29LT Bukra · TPTQ Greta Sans ArabicIBM Plex Sans ArabicNoto Kufi Arabic, Cairo
Mono—Geist Mono · JetBrains Mono—

Arabic is the strongest organic market and cannot get a fallback face. Noto Kufi and Cairo read as "default Arabic web font" to an Arabic-speaking audience — exactly the freelancer signal we are removing. Budget roughly $300–800 for a licensed Arabic web face.

Mono is used only for slate metadata, section numbering and filter labels. There is no Arabic monospace convention and it doesn't matter — slate content is Latin/numeric in both locales, so the system is locale-invariant.

Arabic typesetting rules — non-negotiable

This is where "Arabic as first-class" is actually proven:

  1. Arabic runs ~8–12% larger than Latin at the same optical size. Apply a multiplier on [lang="ar"] inside the type scale.
  2. More line-height — roughly +0.15 over the Latin value, to clear ascenders and diacritics.
  3. Never uppercase Arabic. There is no case in the script. Every text-transform: uppercase must be gated to [lang="en"].
  4. Never letter-space Arabic. It is a connected script; positive tracking breaks the joins and looks like a rendering bug. Gate every letter-spacing rule too.
  5. Use Western Arabic numerals (0–9) throughout, including in AR — standard commercial practice in Jordan, and it keeps slate data consistent across locales.

KColour strategy

One accent, and it is never decorative

--fraise: #C8402F is derived from the brand name — fraise = strawberry — so it carries brand logic rather than being arbitrary.

This restriction is the guardrail. The moment the accent becomes a button fill, the site starts sliding toward a landing page. Enforce it in review.

Contrast

  • #F2EFE9 on #0B0B0C → ~17:1. AAA comfortably.
  • #A8A49C on #0B0B0C → ~8.5:1. AA for the 12px mono slate.
  • #C8402F on #0B0B0C → ~4.6:1. AA for non-text UI and large text. Never body copy. The focus ring adds a 2px offset for shape contrast on top of colour.

Warm white, not neutral. #F2EFE9 rather than #FFFFFF. Pure white next to food photography reads clinical and slightly blue; warm bone sits with the food and makes the page feel lit rather than printed.

MMotion

No animation library in v1

The complete motion vocabulary this site needs: poster↔video crossfade, section reveal on enter, media hover scale, nav overlay, filter state. All five are CSS plus IntersectionObserver.

Framer Motion costs ~35–45 KB gzip and forces "use client" onto layout-level components, which conflicts directly with the Server-Components-by-default requirement. GSAP is ~50 KB+ and is justified only by timeline-sequenced or scroll-scrubbed animation, which this direction explicitly rejects.

The one case that would justify revisiting: a shared-element transition from work card to project detail. Use the native View Transitions API before reaching for a library — progressive enhancement is automatic, and unsupported browsers get an instant navigation.

Principles

  • Slow. 600–800ms reveals. Fast animation reads as UI; slow reads as cinema.
  • One curve. cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1) on everything entering. Never ease-in-out on entrances, never a spring, never a bounce.
  • Opacity + 16px translate is the entire reveal. No scale-in, blur-in, rotate or clip-path wipe.
  • Stagger ≤60ms, ≤3 items. Longer staggers make a grid feel like it's loading badly.
  • Nothing animates twice. Reveals fire once; the observer unobserves.
  • Nothing animates on scroll position. No parallax, no scroll-scrub, no pinned sections — the current AI-portfolio signature, and it wrecks INP.

Reduced motion must be handled in JS, not only CSS

The CSS media query does not stop a <video autoplay>. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reveals apply their end state immediately, all video autoplay is disabled, and every card exposes an explicit play control.

NComponent architecture

The server / client boundary is the whole decision

The naïve approach makes WorkCard a client component because it needs hover — dragging the poster <Image>, the slate markup and the title into the client bundle, multiplied by every card on the page.

Use the children-as-props pattern. A thin client wrapper receives already-rendered server markup:

<HoverPreview          "use client", ~1.5 KB — video element + handlers ONLY
  src={project.previewSrc}
  ratio={project.ratio}
>
  <WorkCardContent />  Server Component: Image poster, slate, title, link
</HoverPreview>

The server component is serialised as an RSC payload and never shipped as JS. The client bundle holds the interaction logic and nothing else.

ONext.js architecture

Fully static, typed content, streamed video

App Router, React Server Components by default, TypeScript strict, Tailwind. Everything is static — generateStaticParams over locales, project slugs and pillar slugs. There is no user-specific content and no real-time data, so full SSG gives CDN-edge HTML, which is most of the performance story before we optimise anything. The only dynamic surface is the contact form's server action.

Content layer — v1

Typed TS data modules, not MDX and not a CMS yet: content/projects.ts, pillars.ts, team.ts, clients.ts, bts.ts, copy/en.ts, copy/ar.ts.

Fully typed, zero runtime cost, tree-shakeable, trivially diffable in git, and it lets us build the whole site with realistic mock data immediately. This is explicitly a v1 decision — the client will want to add work themselves, so designing Project as a clean interface now makes the CMS migration mechanical.

Do not serve MP4 from /public

Full bandwidth rates per byte, no adaptive bitrate, no per-device rendition, no analytics. Full films → Mux (or Cloudflare Stream): HLS, automatic poster extraction at any timestamp, per-title encoding, playback analytics. Mux's frame extraction is what guarantees the seamless poster→video transition in section G. Preview loops → static CDN assets — at <600 KB, adaptive streaming is overhead; a plain MP4 with a faststart atom is faster to first frame. Decide this before build, not after launch.

PInternationalization · EN / AR

The URL decision is the highest-stakes call

The brief says Arabic Reels is the strongest organic page and must not be damaged. The live canonical is fraise.studio/en/, which on a WordPress multilingual setup means Arabic is served from the root.

Recommendation — Option A

Accept about half a day of extra middleware complexity to avoid changing a single URL on the highest-value pages in the estate. The "cleaner" URL structure is an engineering aesthetic; the ranking is revenue.

Conditional on verifying that Arabic is in fact at root today (question W1). If English turns out to be at root, the calculus inverts and Option B is correct.

app/[locale]/...        internal routing, locale ∈ {ar, en}
middleware.ts           rewrites  /reels     → /ar/reels   (URL unchanged)
                        passes    /en/reels  → /en/reels

app/[locale]/layout.tsx sets <html lang dir> server-side, never via client JS. Both locales build from the same route tree, so structural drift between languages is impossible — content equivalence is guaranteed by construction, which is exactly what hreflang reciprocity requires.

i18n library — probably none

~40 lines total
const dictionaries = { en: () => import('@/content/copy/en'),
                       ar: () => import('@/content/copy/ar') };
export const getDictionary = async (locale: Locale) =>
  (await dictionaries[locale]()).default;

With as const, TypeScript catches a missing translation key at compile time — better DX than most i18n libraries provide. Adopt next-intl only if we need per-locale plural rules, date/number formatting or rich-text interpolation. A marketing site whose most complex dynamic value is 2023 does not.

RTL beyond direction

  • Logical properties everywhere, lint-enforced.
  • Mirror directional icons — arrows, chevrons, next-project indicator.
  • Do not mirror media, logos, play buttons, the slate block, Latin brand names, or numerals.
  • The language switcher must swap to the equivalent page, never the homepage. /en/services/reels/ ↔ /services/reels/. Dropping the user on the homepage is the most common bilingual failure and it is a conversion killer.
  • Arabic copy must be written, not translated. Machine-translated Arabic is immediately obvious to a Jordanian client and destroys the positioning.
QSEO architecture

A weak baseline, which is good news

No sitemap declared, none at /sitemap.xml, WordPress default robots rules. The estate ranks on ~52 URLs essentially without technical support — correct fundamentals alone should produce measurable gain.

Metadata

  • generateMetadata per route, per locale. Unique authored title and description on every page — never templated from a slug.
  • metadataBase set once; all canonicals absolute and self-referencing.
  • alternates.languages on every route — Next emits reciprocal hreflang automatically. x-default → the English URL.
  • OG image per project, using the project's own poster frame, generated at build via next/og with the slate baked in. A Fraise-branded still of the actual work shared into WhatsApp — the dominant sharing channel in this market — is worth far more than a generic OG card.

The redirect map — the critical work

  1. Export Search Console → Pages, 16-month window, both locales. The authoritative list of URLs with earned equity.
  2. Crawl the live site with Screaming Frog for the complete inventory including orphans.
  3. Pull server access logs if available, for URLs with traffic but no impressions.
  4. Union, dedupe, and map every URL to a target. No URL left unmapped.

The consolidation trap

Nine granular services collapse to five pillars, so many are many-to-one redirects — and a 301 to a page that does not discuss the old topic is treated by Google as a soft 404, passing little or no equity.

The consolidation only works if the pillar pages absorb the content. That is exactly what the capability-tags block in section E is for: each retired granular service becomes a named, crawlable section on its pillar page, so the query still has a legitimate target. The redirect map and the pillar page content must be designed together, not sequenced.

Reels is priority zero. Every reels-related legacy URL 301s to the Arabic Reels pillar. It ships in phase 1 with content equal or richer than today's. Do not change its URL, do not reduce its copy, do not launch it in the same release as any other AR structural change.

RPerformance

The poster is the LCP element, never the video

This is the whole trick to a video hero with good Core Web Vitals:

  1. Hero poster ships as next/image with priority and fetchPriority="high", AVIF with WebP fallback, sized per breakpoint. It paints as the LCP element.
  2. Hero <video> renders with preload="none" and no src attribute.
  3. After load / on requestIdleCallback, attach src and play().
  4. Crossfade video over the poster in 240ms.

LCP therefore never waits on a video byte. The user sees a full-bleed cinematic frame immediately and it starts moving a beat later — which, incidentally, reads as more premium than a video stuttering into existence.

  • CLS → zero by construction. Every media element declares aspect-ratio from the ratio tokens — which is why the taxonomy doubles as the layout-stability strategy.
  • Fonts. Self-hosted via next/font, aggressively subset — Arabic faces are large. Preload only the two faces used above the fold. Use size-adjust fallback metrics; a badly matched Arabic fallback causes visible reflow.
  • Images. AVIF primary, explicit sizes on every instance — a missing sizes on a full-bleed image is the most common cause of a 3× oversized download.
  • JS. Lightbox and the Mux player are next/dynamic, no SSR. Client components are leaves, never layout.
  • Third parties. Analytics only, strategy="lazyOnload". No chat widget, no heatmap, no tag manager — any one of those can cost more than our entire JS budget.

Lighthouse CI gates every PR against these budgets. On a video-heavy site, performance decays silently unless it is gated.

SAccessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA, with two things most sites miss

  • Semantics. One <h1> per page. Proper landmarks. Work grid is a <ul>; each card an <li> with one link wrapping the whole card. Skip link first in tab order.
  • Focus. :focus-visible ring in the accent with 2px offset — visible over both the near-black ground and bright video frames, because the offset gives shape contrast where colour alone might fail.
  • Keyboard parity for previews. Hover preview fires on :focus-visible too. Non-negotiable.
  • Preview loops are aria-hidden, decorative, no controls — the link carries the name. Full films get native controls, focus trap, Esc to close, focus returned to trigger.
  • Reduced motion handled in CSS and JS; all autoplay off, explicit play control on every card.
  • RTL correctness is an accessibility issue, not just visual. Enforce with a custom lint rule banning physical-direction utilities outside an allowlist.

Captions are a content deliverable with real cost

WCAG 1.2.2 requires captions for prerecorded audio. Fraise's TVCs have Arabic voiceover and music, so full films need caption tracks in Arabic and English. Raise this with the client now, not at launch.

VRisks

Ranked by expected damage

01Highest stakes

Arabic URL migration damages the Reels page

Mitigation: preserve AR at root (Option A); 1:1 redirect mapping; do not change that page's content, title or URL in the launch release; monitor it individually in GSC daily for two weeks.

02Most likely to actually bite

Insufficient high-quality footage

This entire direction — full-bleed frames, big video, editorial cadence — depends on having enough excellent assets. Eight nameable clients and five BTS films is a thin inventory for a grid built on 2.39:1 full-bleed rows. With only 12–15 genuinely premium pieces, a big-video grid looks emptier and weaker than the current small-thumbnail grid.

Mitigation: asset audit is the first task in phase 0. If the count is low, reduce the number of rows and increase the size of each rather than padding, and lean harder on BTS and process content. Better eight pieces beautifully than twenty at three quality tiers.

03Content, not code

The team roster undermines the studio positioning

Three of six members share the surname Aqraa, and one is listed as an e-commerce consultant and entrepreneur. On a page whose explicit job is to prove "this is a production company, not one person with a camera", a non-production business role on the crew list works directly against the goal, and a visibly family-weighted roster reads as a small family business rather than a studio. This is an observation about the page, not a criticism of the people.

Mitigation, in order of preference: (a) restructure the page around production departments — Direction, Cinematography, Food Styling, Post — with people listed under capabilities, so the roster reads as crew structure; (b) move the commercial role to a separate Leadership block; (c) credit regular freelance collaborators. This needs a client conversation and it matters more to the studio-vs-freelancer objective than any design decision in this document.

04Cost

Video bandwidth on Vercel

Serving MP4 from /public at studio-site traffic produces a surprising invoice. Mitigation: Mux / Cloudflare Stream decision in phase 0.

05SEO

Many-to-one redirects become soft 404s

Mitigation: pillar pages absorb the retired services' content as capability sections. Redirect map and pillar copy designed together.

06Expectation

No CMS in v1

The client will want to add work without a developer. Mitigation: be explicit that v1 is developer-updated, design Project as a clean interface, scope Sanity or Payload as phase 8. Do not let this quietly become an unmanaged expectation.

07Content

Arabic copy quality

Translated-sounding Arabic destroys premium positioning with the primary audience faster than any design flaw. Mitigation: budget an Arabic copywriter; Arabic is authored, not translated.

08Market

Mobile data cost

Mitigation: one decoder maximum, saveData honoured, <600 KB preview budget, posters-only on 2G.

09Budget

Arabic font licensing

Good Arabic type is expensive and web licences are often traffic-tiered. Mitigation: decide in phase 0; IBM Plex Sans Arabic is a credible zero-cost fallback that won't embarrass the design.

10Process

Requests that erode the direction

"Can we add a gradient here?" "Can the button be red?" "Can we add a testimonial slider?" Mitigation: the forbidden-uses list in K and the motion principles in M exist to be cited in review. Written constraints are easier to defend than taste.

Build it in Arabic RTL as the primary demo

The recommendation I'd defend hardest, and deliberately contrarian. Arabic is the strongest organic market — demo in the language of the audience that converts. It proves the hardest technical requirement on day one: anyone can demo an English studio site, but a well-set RTL cinematic site with correct Arabic typography, mirrored chrome and unmirrored media is a far stronger competence signal. And every competing pitch will demo in English. Ship both locales — present Arabic first.

Must be real, not placeholder

  • Real footage from the actual client roster. Lorem-ipsum video kills this direction outright — the design's whole thesis is that the work is the hero. If the work is stock, there is nothing to evaluate.
  • Real client names.
  • Real hover behaviour, with the concurrency cap and intent delay working.
  • Deployed to Vercel with a real Lighthouse score visible.

Explicitly out of scope: team, BTS, case studies, contact backend, CMS, the full redirect map, the other four pillars — noted as scoped-and-costed rather than silently omitted.

The bar to judge it against

Open the deployed URL cold on a phone and ask: within three seconds and zero reading, is it obvious that this is a production company that shoots food? If the answer needs a caption, the direction has failed — and we change it at phase 3, not at launch.
